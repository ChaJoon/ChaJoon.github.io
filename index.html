Wireless RFID E-Textile Breathing Sensor

First-class mark

Group Design Project · 2025–26

ANSYS HFSSKiCadMultisimRFIDFlexible PCB

Designed a battery-free capacitive sensor (a capaciflector) that reads breathing through clothing and reports it over passive RFID. The RFID chip's datasheet only covered two-plate capacitors, so I worked out how to drive a three-electrode sensor from a single pin. Tested from 80 to 160 cm at a paced 6 breaths per minute, it gave the clearest breathing pattern at 100 cm.

Read more
  • Team and my part: team of 5; I led the capaciflector sensor, end to end.
  • Problem: continuous breathing monitoring outside intensive care, with no battery on the patient.
  • What I did: simulated the sensing principle in Multisim; designed and simulated the antenna in ANSYS HFSS, including the effect of the human body; produced ten flexible-PCB variants in KiCad and fixed manufacturing-rule violations; assembled the tags by hand.
  • Interface from first principles: routed the ground plane to the sensing pin so the chip drove both reflector and sensor electrodes. This went into the final design.
  • When fabrication failed: screen-printed sensors shorted from air bubbles in the dielectric, twice. I switched to Kapton–copper clad, which worked reliably.
  • Debugging: long wires made the data noisy; a compact version lost read range from antenna coupling; a smaller sensor geometry fixed both.
  • Result: tested from 80 to 160 cm with breathing paced at 6 per minute by a metronome. The pattern was clear at 80–100 cm, still recognisable at 120 cm, and weaker beyond. 100 cm was the best compromise between patient comfort and signal, with an average peak-to-peak swing of 75 raw counts.
  • What I'd change: test the fabrication route in week 1, not week 7.

NISC Processor on FPGA

First-class mark

Individual coursework · 2026

SystemVerilogQuestaIntel QuartusCyclone V

Built a No-Instruction-Set-Computer (NISC) processor that runs a 5-tap Gaussian smoothing filter on a 256-sample waveform. Control signals are bit fields in the instruction word, so there is no decoder logic. Final cost: 63 logic elements and one DSP multiplier, verified in simulation and on hardware.

Read more
  • Problem: minimise an area cost figure for a fixed signal-processing task on a Cyclone V.
  • Key decision: NISC instead of an encoded-instruction design. A slightly wider program ROM removes all decoder logic and wins on area.
  • Result: 9-instruction program, 7 clock cycles per output sample, all 8 test cases passed in Questa (positive, negative, boundary indices), confirmed on the DE1-SoC board.
  • What I'd change: add a self-checking testbench against a Python reference model rather than hand-checked cases.

Accelerometer Signal Conditioning for Engine Vibration

First-class mark

Individual coursework · 2026

NI MultisimAnalogue designHand calculation

Designed the interface between a piezoresistive accelerometer and an engine controller on a single 5 V supply. My first differential amplifier loaded the sensor bridge, with 78% signal loss predicted and 60% in simulation. An instrumentation amplifier cut it to 0.35%, and the full chain met the 100 ± 5 mV/g target.

Read more
  • Chain: Wheatstone bridge model → three-op-amp instrumentation amplifier → cascaded 2nd-order Butterworth filters giving a 20–170 Hz passband (1,200–10,200 RPM).
  • Diagnosis, not retuning: traced the loss to bridge output impedance versus amplifier input impedance. Raising resistors 10× only reduced loss to 26%, so I changed topology.
  • Result: 96–110 mV/g, ±1.2 dB passband flatness, current cut 7.1% to 2.87 mA. All values calculated by hand, then checked in Multisim.
  • What I'd change: build it on a protoboard to see real op-amp offsets.

NEM Resonator Characterisation

First-class mark

Individual coursework · 2026

Lock-in amplifierPythonCOMSOLMATLAB

Measured a 2 µm silicon nanoelectromechanical (NEM) beam resonator with lock-in detection and fitted its response in Python. Resonance sat near 98 MHz with a quality factor of 270–400. A COMSOL model matched within 3%, and a power sweep revealed a 3 MHz Duffing stiffening shift.

Read more
  • Method: 1-ω mixing measurement under back-gate bias 0–2 V and drive power 0 to −8 dB; Lorentzian fits for frequency and Q.
  • Model: COMSOL eigenfrequency study of a pure-silicon beam (99.13 MHz) and a Si/SiO₂ core-shell beam (95.02 MHz).
  • Also: a critical review of a published nonlinear-resonance modelling method on identical devices.
  • What I'd change: fit the Duffing model directly instead of tracking peak shift.

Piezoelectric Accelerometer Package

First-class mark

Individual coursework · 2026

SolidWorks3D printingNI MultisimLCR meter

Designed a 3D-printed housing and a two-stage charge amplifier (gain 20) for a piezoelectric disc, then swept it from 0.5 Hz to 5 kHz on a shaker at three masses. The main peak sat near 850 Hz against a 4.2 kHz datasheet value. I traced it to the shaker's own resonance and found the disc's mass-shifted peaks at 2.5–2.7 kHz.

Read more
  • Sensor check: characterised the disc on an LCR meter (18.44 nF at 1 kHz, within datasheet tolerance).
  • Simulation versus measurement: explained the gap through protoboard parasitics, series resistors replacing one 10 MΩ part, and probe loading.
  • What I'd change: use a reference accelerometer to separate the shaker's response from the sensor's.

Photodiode Transimpedance Amplifier Design Review

Individual take-home technical challenge for a photonics company · 2026

Analogue designNoise analysisLoop stabilityPCB review

Reviewed a two-stage photodiode transimpedance amplifier (TIA) board across schematic, layout and bring-up. I found a repeated placeholder pattern that disabled the feedback and compensation networks, and a misplaced resistor on the sensitive input node. I also proposed a step-by-step bring-up and test plan for gain, bandwidth, noise and stability.

Read more
  • Approach: tied input capacitance, feedback resistance, compensation, bandwidth and noise together instead of checking each alone; checked calculations against manufacturer data.
  • Findings: the placeholder faults, a summing-node resistor that likely belonged on the guard-buffer output, marginal phase margin in both stages, and missing test points.
  • Testability: proposed current-injection points and a validation sequence.
  • What I'd change, from the reviewers' feedback: lead with a one-page list of board-stopping issues before secondary findings; state the assumptions behind any component recommendation; confirm layout findings in the native PCB viewer before reporting them.

2000 MW HVDC Subsea Interconnector Design Study

Group design study plus individual converter simulations · 2026

MATLAB/SimulinkPythonPower electronicsHVDC

Selected the converter topology, ratings, cable and protection for a 200 km, 2000 MW subsea High-Voltage Direct Current (HVDC) link between two asynchronous grids. I wrote the power-quality and filtering design for a ±525 kV bipole with half-bridge Modular Multilevel Converters (MMC). Separately, I simulated line-commutated and voltage-source converters in MATLAB/Simulink.

Read more
  • Team and my part: team of 6; I owned power quality and filtering.
  • Group study: design decisions from case studies of operating links. My section showed the MMC's staircase output keeps AC-side distortion within IEEE 519 without tuned filter banks, and sized the phase reactor.
  • Individual simulations: Line-Commutated Converter (LCC) voltage, commutation overlap and harmonics; designed 5th, 7th and damped AC filters that cut total harmonic distortion from 27.27% to 7.13%; voltage-source converter power flow and a 3-level converter. Plots produced in Python.
  • Next step we identified: electromagnetic transient studies of converter–grid resonance before finalising reactors.
